Previous model (2026-10-27, now in versions/pre-ambient-copy/): soft-body jelly, replacing the lava-lamp visuals at the user's request. Reference: https://github.com/makoto-lee/softbody-physics-simulation (spring-mass nodes of unit mass, Hooke springs k 0.15 with 0.9 force decay, 0.995 air resistance per frame, anti-self-collapse, point-in-polygon collision projected to the nearest edge; it cites Matyka's pressure soft-body paper). Concepts adapted, no code copied.

Six small circular bodies (radius ~32–40 px), each a ring of 16 nodes braced by ring, skip-one and chord springs, with area-restoring pressure. Each follows its own random path (~20 px/s): an endless Catmull-Rom curve through random points spread over the disc, biased outward to reach the rim, from a per-body random stream. Segment durations are proportional to length (PATH_PACE 20 px/s, points at least 0.5 disc radii apart), so the target never crawls; the earlier fixed 14 s per segment made bodies park for 10+ s on short segments. The body seeks a target sliding along the path, loosely, plus a free random drift (Ornstein-Uhlenbeck velocity from a second per-body stream, ~3 s correlation); the Free wander slider (0–100%, default 60%) scales the drift and weakens the seek. Drift never pushes backwards against the path, so it can't stall a body. Collisions knock a body off but never change the path, its timing or the drift, so it rejoins the same curve. The only driving force is this steering push (wanted velocity minus current velocity, per node); there is no attraction between bodies. Personal space (2026-10-28, user saw blobs gathering into one still mass behind a stuck one): within 1.7× the touching distance a body's wanted velocity heading into a neighbour is turned sideways (slide around) and a soft nudge moves the two apart; near the rim the outward part is turned back inward; body contacts bounce (restitution 0.3) instead of removing approach speed. Measured over 3 min with 6 bodies: 3+ blob clumps ~5% of the time (was ~50%), contacts last 3–8 s (was 20–40), no body still for more than ~3 s (was up to 13). Bodies also morph on their own: chord rest lengths follow a slowly rotating oval plus trefoil. Collisions with each other and with the round glass dent the outline and spring back. Live tuning sliders (with Reset): spring stiffness k 0.02–0.40 (default 0.15); size of blobs ×0.5–2 of ~36 px radius (rescales rest lengths and area); collision deformation 0–100% (default 75%: share of each wall/body contact taken as a local dent; the rest pushes the whole body, so 0 = firm push, no dent; this is separate from stiffness, which sets how hard the shape springs back); number of blobs 1–10 (default 6; adds in free space, removes newest); gravity 0–0.05 px/frame² (default off; reference uses 0.025); translucency 0–90% (default opaque; renderer only, fades each body and its soft border); speed ×0–8 of path pace (0 = hold position on the path; ~22 px/s at ×1, ~65 at ×3, ~165 at ×8; steering gain scales with speed so bodies keep up); wobble 0–30% self-morph (default 11%). Fixed 60 Hz frames, 4 substeps. Background #594F47. Drawn as solid infill with a blurry, translucent border, from a user reference image (2026-10-28): one flat colour, the ambient owner teal #00E2B7, with no outline, gloss or gradient; the border runs from 9 px outside to 9 px inside the outline (capped at a quarter of the body radius) as 8 non-overlapping even-odd bands on a smoothstep alpha ramp, then a solid core; the whole scene drawn into a 240 × 240 buffer and smoothed up to the native 480 × 480 round canvas. Slime (2026-10-28, user asked for more slimy behaviour that reduces in proportion to how long the user shakes): live sliminess = Sliminess slider (0–100%, default 80%) × (1 − shake drain). It softens springs (up to −45%) and adds internal viscosity, eases pressure (up to −60%), adds plasticity (spring rest lengths creep toward the current length and relax slowly, so dents linger ~1 s at full slime vs ~0.1 s plain), and makes touching nodes stick: strands between bodies (up to 3 per pair) and to the glass (up to 2 per body) pull back only when stretched, snap after 10–55 px of stretch or 1–4 s, and a pair can't re-stick for 1.2 s. The renderer draws strands behind the bodies as soft round strokes (14 px wide, thinning as they stretch). Shake drain (ShakeDrain in softbody.mjs): while held with intensity > 0.12, drain rises linearly by 1/10 per second of shaking (fully firm after 10 s); holding still pauses it; after release it seeps back linearly over 30 s from fully drained. Recovery speed was my choice; the user only specified the drain. interaction-v15.mjs still drives the shared WebSocket protocol (unchanged); app.js reads its held and intensity for the drain.
