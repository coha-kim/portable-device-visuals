# Portable Device Visuals

Harvey palm-device prototype on the native 480 × 480 round display: fifty small jelly soft bodies (radius about 13 px; up to 200) on #CCCCCC. Each is filled with one solid colour, orange #F47839 by default, solid at rest, with no highlight or gradient, hollowing into an outline as the user interacts (the **Colour** slider changes the hue: every hue keeps the teal's saturation and lightness, so all choices are equally vivid on the background, and the slider's readout shows a swatch and hex code). Edges are crisp: each blob is a single fill drawn straight onto the 480 × 480 canvas, with no blur or soft border.

**Real size:** the **Display size** box above the sliders has a **Real size (53.28 mm diameter)** checkbox. A browser can't measure a screen's physical size (CSS millimetres assume 96 px per inch; a MacBook screen shows about 127), so it needs a one-time calibration:

1. Hold a bank card (85.60 mm wide) against the screen.
2. Drag **Screen calibration** until the card outline matches the card.

The calibration and the checkbox are remembered in that browser. Keep the browser zoom at 100%. The canvas still renders 480 × 480 pixels and is scaled to fit.

## Physics

`softbody.mjs` follows lisyarus, [Making a 2D soft-body physics engine](https://lisyarus.github.io/blog/posts/soft-body-physics.html), driven only by the device's own gravity. Units are px and seconds, in fixed 1/240 s steps.

Rules from the article:

- **Point masses:** each body is a ring of 16 unit-mass points, integrated with symplectic Euler (forces update velocities, then velocities update positions).
- **Shape matching:** instead of a spring mesh, each body has a rest shape. Every step the best-fit rotation θ = −atan2(Σ r×q, Σ r·q) places the rest shape on the current centre of mass, and a spring pulls each point toward its matched position. This keeps the shape without turning inside out.
- **Damping:** each point's velocity is blended toward the body's rigid motion (centre velocity plus average spin) by 1 − exp(−damping·dt). Wobble dies down, but the glide and spin survive.
- **Stability:** stiffness·dt² stays well below 1 (at most 0.05).
- **Collision detection:** each point checks the round glass and every other nearby body. Only the collision with the largest penetration depth is resolved.
- **Collision resolution:** the point is pushed out along the normal (no sinking). The normal velocity becomes −elasticity·vn and the tangential velocity is multiplied by exp(−friction·dt). Velocity is only resolved while the point moves into the collider. Against another body the correction is shared with that edge's two points, so momentum is kept.

Modifications:

- **Device gravity is the only driving force.** Tilting or moving the device gives every blob the same acceleration (`gravity.mjs`): the accelerometer's reading includes gravity, and the contents feel the opposite of it. Lying level and still, nothing moves: blobs coast to a stop under drag and stay put. Below 0.05 g (a device on a table, slightly off level) counts as level.
- **Perfect circles at rest:** the rest shape is an exact circle (wobble defaults to 0). Blobs squash on contact through the same shape-matching springs and turn round again once free.
- **Exit along the radius:** a point inside another blob leaves along the line from that blob's centre, not through the nearest edge. Under a pile's weight the nearest-edge rule let points exit through the far side and fold blobs inside out.
- **Area-keeping pressure:** pushes a squashed outline back out, so heavy piles can't crush a blob flat.
- **Resting contact:** contacts slower than 20 px/s don't bounce, and there is no extra contact gap. Together these stop piles jiggling: a pile under a steady tilt drifts at most about 1 px/s, under 0.2 mm/s on the panel.
- **Speed control:** drag (exp(−drag·dt)) lets blobs settle, and a top speed caps each blob's centre speed. The wobble about the centre is untouched.
- **Settling:** a blob touching something without a break for 0.4 s counts as resting, and its wobble is damped at least 30 /s. A bounce is a brief contact, so it keeps the livelier slider damping. (At 10 /s the edges where resting blobs touch still flickered by up to a few px a frame; solid blobs hide that, but outlines after shaking made it show as jitter.) Without this a deep 50-blob pile kept vibrating in place at about 9 px/s; it now rests at about 2 px/s (0.2 mm/s on the panel).
- **Body-level elasticity:** bouncing single points barely changes how bodies rebound. The shape springs store the impact and push them apart again: 65 px/s at elasticity 1 and 58 at 0, before this rule. So while two bodies touch, or a body touches the glass, their parting speed is capped at elasticity × the speed they met at. The cap eases off by 10 px/s per second of contact, so inelastic contacts still come apart. Measured rebound from an 80 px/s head-on meeting: 65, 40 and 8 px/s at elasticity 1, 0.5 and 0.

## Controls

The page is unstyled, with browser-default controls on white. Only the round display has styling. Jelly sliders, with Reset:

| Shape stiffness | 50–3000 /s² | 2450 |
| Wobble damping | 0–20 /s | 13.2 |
| Elasticity | 0–2 | 0.99 (shaking lowers it to 0.12). Sets the rebound of any impact over 60 px/s to exactly elasticity × the approach speed: a blob dropped on the glass keeps about 1% at 0, 46% at 0.5, 85% at 1, 128% at 1.5 and 171% at 2. Above about 1.5 blobs gain energy on every bounce, so a pile never settles. |
| Friction (on contact) | 0–50 /s | 0.2 (shaking raises it to 9) |
| Gravity strength | 0–2000 px/s² per g | 1002 |
| Max speed | 10–1000 px/s | 600 |

**Colour edit (this page only, `colour-edit.mjs`):** press a blob for 3 s without moving more than 14 px (a thin black arc fills around it from 0.3 s). The blob is spared the press's push, though the tilt still moves it. A colour wheel opens around the border, in the Colour slider's colours, with a black line from the current colour to the blob that follows it as it moves. Press the wheel, or slide out to it without lifting, and move along it to pick a colour. Pull in from the wheel to stretch a sling with a drop of colour at the fingertip, and let go: the drop homes in on the blob, nudges it, and the new colour spreads from there through the whole population (one colour per person, so the slider updates too). A pull under 24 px fires nothing and leaves the wheel open. A press inside the wheel or Escape closes it.

**Gravity and push (stands in for the motion sensor):** after the mouse interaction in Magnum's [fluidsimulation2d](https://github.com/mosra/magnum-examples/tree/master/src/fluidsimulation2d) (its `addRepulsiveVelocity` and constant gravity), **pressing on the round display** makes that point the origin of the force:
- **Gravity** points away from the press, so the opposite side becomes the ground. It is stronger the further from the centre you press (up to 0.6 g at the rim) and stays after you let go, like a plate left tilted. A press near the centre, or the **Level** button, levels it.
- **A push** kicks every blob away from the press by push × (1 − distance/reach)². Blobs close to the press fly off and bounce off the ground, mid-distance ones roll, and distant ones (already lying on the ground) barely move.
- **Dragging** stirs the blobs near the stroke along with the pointer.

A phone uses its motion sensor instead once **Use phone motion → Enable motion** is on. The readout shows the gravity in g and where it comes from. The **Hold + shake test** also shakes the device virtually.

Defaults were tuned for livelier bounces (stiffness 1000, wobble damping 1.5): a blob dropped onto bare glass keeps about 72% of its speed (53% before). A blob landing on the pile is cushioned by the jelly below it.

At 0.5 g a falling pile settles at the low side within about 5 s. When the device is level again, blobs stop within about 5 s and return to perfect circles.

**Shaking:** as the user interacts, the blobs go from bouncy and slippery to dull and clingy, in proportion to how long the shaking lasts. Each second of held shaking adds 1/10 of drain, and elasticity, friction and drag move from their sliders toward 0.12, 9 /s and 0.72 /s (`DRAINED` in `softbody.mjs`). Shaking also lowers the top speed by up to 60%. Holding still pauses the drain, and after release it seeps back 60× as slowly as it built up (3 s of shaking takes 3 min to recover; a full 10 s takes 10 min). The status line shows live and slider elasticity. Shaking still drives the shared ambient protocol.

**Fill to outline:** at rest every blob is a solid disc. While the user interacts (held shaking), the blobs hollow into outlines, with the outline thickness inversely proportional to how long the interaction has lasted: radius × 1 s / (1 s + t). That is the full radius (solid) at 0 s, half at 1 s, a quarter at 3 s and about 2 px at 10 s, never under 1.5 px. As the interaction time seeps back after release (60× slower: 3 s of shaking takes 3 min), they fill in again. The status line shows the current outline width. This replaces the Translucency slider.

## Colour transfer (prepared, not yet on hardware)

Picking a colour and then tapping the portable near the ambient display sends that colour to the ambient display, where it becomes this portable's population colour. Built so far:
- the protocol message (`colour`, additive to v1);
- the relay server storing it and broadcasting `snapshot.colours`;
- `link.sendColour()`;
- the tap rules in `proximity.mjs` (`NearDetector`: smoothed Bluetooth signal strength, dwell, hysteresis, cooldown);

## Exhibition web page

`exhibit.html` is the public version for the exhibition: the same physics, without the tuning controls, on a #F4F2EE ground with very little text (the wordmark, the one intro line, and the Hold to shake / Use phone motion buttons). The round screen sits beside a rounded camera panel, joined by the battery indicator.

- **Press** the screen to tip it (and push the blobs near the press). **Hold the mouse down and shake it** to shake the device: the pointer's acceleration feeds the same shake model as the phone's sensor, and the blobs feel the opposite. **Hold to shake** (or the space bar) shakes it for you. On a phone, **Use phone motion** turns on tilt, and holding the screen while shaking drains it.
- **Faster recovery for visitors:** 1 s of shaking takes 6 s to come back (3 s → 18 s, a full shake → 1 min), against 60× on the device. After a minute with nobody, the plate levels.
- **Battery indicator:** an orange semicircle on the screen's right, full from bottom to top at the baseline. As the battery drains its end slides down the arc; a level orange line runs from the end to the camera panel, and the panel slides with it (kept inside the stage). Below 760 px wide the camera is hidden and the semicircle stays.
- **Camera (`presence-mirror.mjs`):** off until the visitor taps **Show me**. The visitor is cut out with MediaPipe's selfie segmenter (loaded from jsDelivr and Google's model storage, run in the browser, nothing recorded or sent) and drawn mirrored at an opacity of 1 − drain: fully there at the baseline, fading as they shake, back as it recovers. The room behind them is learned as they move, over the plain panel colour, so they fade into the room, never into a ghost of themselves. Without segmentation the whole image fades.
- **Ambient display:** open it from the bridge home page (`npm run bridge`, then **Open the exhibition web page**). It sends the population colour, always Harvey orange #F47839 (visitors can't change it), and hold, shake intensity and release, like the portable.
- **3D bubbles (this page only):** `renderer-3d.mjs` draws the same 2D physics as glossy orange bubbles in WebGL2. The bodies' outlines are joined with a smooth union, so touching bubbles meet in a rounded neck rather than a crease; each gets a dome height, a sharp window highlight, a faint second reflection, a deeper edge and a soft warm shadow on the glass. As the battery drains each bubble hollows into a clear film inside an orange wall (the wall is the outline width), so a drained pile looks like soap-bubble foam with shared walls. About 0.3 ms a frame at up to 2× pixel density. Without WebGL2 (or half-float targets) it falls back to the flat renderer.
- **Sticky, high surface tension:** the page's physics has stiffer shapes (1800 /s²) and sticky contacts (`SoftBodyWorld({stick})`, off on the device): a point within 4 px of another bubble's outline is pulled in (700 px/s² at contact) and the closing speed damped, so bubbles cling and flatten into a shared wall. A 60 px/s nudge doesn't part a pair; 240 px/s does. Stickiness fades with the drain, so a flat battery's bubbles stop merging. Physics with it takes about 1.7 ms a frame.
- The wordmark is cropped from the layout sketch (`assets/harvey-wordmark-sketch.png`) until the master vector is in the design system. Colours and type follow the Harvey design system (yellow ground, orange, ink; DM Sans and IBM Plex Mono).

## Drain page (web)

`drain.html` (with `drain.mjs` and `pocket-softbody.mjs`): the Pocket's tipping, for a mouse or trackpad. The page is plain #F4F2EE with the blobs in Harvey orange #F47839 and nothing else. Press and drag to pinch it: a soft funnel with gathered folds appears where you pressed, and that point becomes the drain, the origin of the gravity, so every blob is pulled into it (`pocket-softbody.mjs` is a port of `firmware/harvey_pocket/softbody.cpp` with a rectangular page for the glass and gravity pulling toward a point instead of one tilt direction). How far you pull from the press is how far the device is tipped: 0.3 × the page's short side is 60°, the 10° dead zone about a twentieth of it. The rest follows the firmware: the drain rises with the tip (curve 1.5, a full battery in 3 s at full pull), the blobs melt and merge while held (squish 0.3 → 1 over 4 s of full tipping), the colour is sucked out from the far side toward the drain (front strength 2, smoothstepped), and after release the page levels out over 0.3 s and the blobs keep the low-battery spacing, bounce and speed. Three quick presses reset. Recovery is 6× (`?recovery=60` matches the device). Drained colour mutes to its own grey and then recedes 45% toward the page, not darkens as on the device's dark glass (`?recede=`). Other parameters: `?n=` blobs (up to 7), `?r=` radius in device px, `?pull=`, `?colour=`, `?bg=`. `d` shows live values, `r` resets. Needs WebGL2; the page's short side is the device's 480 px. Not connected to the relay yet.

## Tray page (web)

## Pull page (web)

`pull.html` (with `pull.mjs`, self-contained): two halves. Left, your blobs: three #B2D8E5 blobs bonded in a chain on Harvey blue #3F7ABB, drifting slowly, drawn as one shared field with a soft dark halo outside the edge. Grab one and pull: the bonds are strong and sticky (force grows with the stretch up to 0.45 R, the others drag at 5 /s), so a slow pull brings the whole cluster along and a firm one (about 500 px/s or more) stretches a neck between them until it snaps at 2.8 R; the pointer going 3.4 R from the partner also snaps it, so pulling off the edge works. On the snap both ends recoil and squash, then wobble back (a jelly shape spring). When the chain is no longer one group, the right half reacts at once: of the three populations (#B2D8E5 yours, #C2E596, #3F7ABB), yours breaks away from the others, which close up. The green speech bubble rides on your top blob (lagging on a spring) as a status, and swaps to a low-battery line on the snap ("I just want to be in bed.", "Talked out for today." …), back to a sociable one on rejoining. Apart, the blobs keep a gap for 16 s (`?apart=`), then find each other and stick on contact; you can also push a blob back into the others after the first 1.2 s. Everything rejoins on both halves. Not tied to the Pocket's physics or drain, and not connected to the relay.

## On the board

`firmware/harvey_pocket/` runs the blobs on the real ESP32-S3-Touch-LCD-2.1 (flashed over USB-C; no SD card needed). `softbody.cpp` is a C++ port of `softbody.mjs`; the display, touch, I2C and sensor drivers are Waveshare's, from their example package.

- **Input:** read 200 times a second. The QMI8658's gyro turns the gravity estimate at once and the accelerometer pulls it true over 0.5 s while it reads about 1 g, so tilt is quick and movement doesn't pollute it. What the accelerometer feels beyond gravity sloshes the blobs. Touch only sets the colour; it doesn't move the blobs. Tipping the screen while holding the button drains (see **Tipping**), and it seeps back 60× as slowly after release. The sensor's axes are set for the enclosure, with the button on top and the USB port at the bottom (`ACCEL_SCREEN_X` / `ACCEL_SCREEN_Y` at the top of the sketch).
- **Feel:** the same baseline and drained values as the browser, except three 75 px blobs (not fifty 13 px ones), softer shapes (stiffness 1500 and damping 10, not 2450 and 13.2), a level zone of 0.2 g (a board on a desk reads up to about 0.15 g), max speed 300 px/s (not 600) and tilt capped at 0.3 g: real tilting drives blobs harder than a mouse, and fast 13 px blobs otherwise sink more than halfway into each other. Steps are 1/190 s rather than 1/240 s, which is what the board sustains in a full pile; stiffness·dt² is 0.068, over the 0.05 guideline but inside the tests' 0.1 bound, and a two-minute 2 g stress run on the Mac stayed stable.
- **Contact:** the rebound after an impact is eased in over 60 ms rather than added in one step, and points don't bounce off the glass individually (the browser still does both the old way). A one-step kick read as stutter on walls and blob collisions; the biggest jolt in a moving pile fell from 448 to 164 px/s per frame² in simulation, with bounce kept (87% head-on, 93% off the glass).
- **Speed:** about 58 fps (the panel's refresh rate), physics in real time at 60–85% of one core. Physics runs on core 0 from internal RAM. Core 1 takes the panel's refresh interrupt, draws in 48-row bands in internal RAM, and copies only the 16-px tiles holding a blob now or in that buffer's last frame, because PSRAM writes are the limit. An SD card wouldn't help: nothing reads from it.
- **Colour wheel:** press a blob for 3 s for the wheel, as on the prototype: pick a colour, pull away and let go to sling it into the blob, and it spreads through the population (`colour_edit.h`, a port of `colour-edit.mjs`). The colour isn't remembered after a restart.
- **Not on the board yet:** the sliders, the link to the ambient display, and edge smoothing (edges are aliased at about 229 pixels per inch).
- **Live values:** `python3 firmware/dashboard/server.py` (or preview "dashboard"), then http://127.0.0.1:8041/. It shows tilt, tip, drain, touch, colour edit, frame rate and physics load, with a gravity dial and 30 s charts. It holds the serial port, so stop it before flashing. The board streams 20 lines a second only while the dashboard asks, and frame rate reads about 50 fps with it connected (58 was last measured without it, before the colour wheel).
- **Look:** a #1C1C1C background (set by eye to match Harvey Lounge's screen, whose value is #2F302F) and the Lounge's wax field, with #3F7ABB as the baseline colour. Blobs add to a shared field that sets a soft edge and a barely lighter middle, so close blobs fuse into one shape; as merging fades, the field falls to the strongest blob alone. Shaded at half resolution and scaled up, with a fine dither. Rows go straight to the frame buffer unless the colour wheel is open.
- **Baseline movement:** three blobs with vertex forces: skin springs along the outline, area pressure, a soft pull to the rest shape (stiffness 190, damping 4) and stretch along their flight (0.45), so a wall hit squashes the whole blob to about 2×. At baseline each rest shape slowly wobbles out of round, up to 9% in an oval and about 4.5% in a trefoil, turning and swelling at each blob's own rate (on average about 6% out of round while drifting, 2% before). The wobble fades with the drain and while held, so low-capacity blobs settle round and still. Tilt up to 0.6 g, gravity 1100 px/s², top speed 700 px/s, all run 1.6× faster than real time at baseline, easing to real time with the drain. Level, each blob drifts along its own free path over the whole plate: three slow sines per axis at unrelated rates, with phases from the boot seed, so no path repeats or keeps to one area (in simulation each blob visits about 70% of the plate in five minutes, against 15% for the old orbit paths). A path whose target comes within 60 px of another blob's outline bends gently away, so they meet when paths really cross (touching about 28% of the time, around 25 meetings in five minutes). When they meet they merge (skin reaches across, up to 34 px of overlap) and part again as the paths lead away. Paths are assigned to the nearest blob only when the board comes back to level.
- **Merges don't last while tilted:** only while the board is tilted (past the level zone) without the button held, a pair within 12 px for 1.5 s has its paths steered apart until 30 px clear, at a gentle fixed speed, so it reads as a change of course. The limit is 1.5 s ÷ (1 + seconds of drain), so it shrinks the lower the social battery. Level, merges last as long as the drifting paths keep the pair together (in simulation about 6–12 s on average, up to about a minute) and part when the paths lead away; held, there's no limit either.
- **Lower social battery:** with the drain, merging falls to nothing (near the lowest point blobs repel each other from 70 px away, and keep at least 2 px apart, turning aside before they touch), hits lose their bounce so walls change direction rather than rebound, speed falls, and blobs still squash as a whole but heavy damping stops them springing back, and the colour's saturation falls all the way to its own grey with the hue kept while it darkens to half its brightness (stronger than Harvey Lounge's 80% mute). All of this shows on the board in real time while the button is held; Harvey Lounge gets it on release.
- **Rings:** a thin ring around the panel for 1.5 s: white on a reset, white after a release the relay confirmed, red if it wasn't sent or confirmed within 1.5 s. Holds under 0.4 s show no send ring. **Low battery:** the battery's voltage (GPIO 4, through the board's divide-by-3 divider, smoothed over about 10 s) below 3.5 V pulses the ring red every 2 s, until it's back above 3.6 V; a send or reset ring shows over it. It's drawn straight into the frame buffer, so the frame rate holds (about 22 fps with or without it; drawn the way the send ring is, it fell to 15). The serial line shows the voltage.
- **Tipping:** while the button is held, the drain rises with how far the screen has turned from where it faced at the press, in any direction (from the fused gravity, so the gyroscope tracks it at once). Under 10° is just holding; from there it quickens (to the power 1.5) to its fastest at 60° and beyond, where a full battery empties in 3 s (about 12 s at 30°). While held the blobs melt like jelly in a container: a squish starts at 0.3 on the press and rises to 1 over 4 s of full tipping, taking the pull to their rest shape away entirely, the skin springs to 30%, the damping (and the resting settle) to 30%, and pulling 2.5× as hard with gravity, so the wax slumps flat along the glass like a raw egg (the anti-tearing bend stays: softening it tore the outline in simulation). While held they merge whatever the drain, the 1.5 s merge limit is off and so is the low-battery distance (the 2 px gap, the avoiding and the repelling); the squish eases back over 0.8 s after release, and the limit and spacing return with it. The colour drains toward where it's tipped: each pixel's drain is drain × (1 + w) − w × (1 − u), with u from 0 at the wax's tipped edge to 1 at its far edge, so the far side greys first and the grey creeps toward the low side as a soft front; w (up to 2) eases in over 0.3 s while held and out over 1.5 s after release, when the colour evens out to the plain drain (32 muted ramps, the front eased with a smoothstep so it has no hard edge, dithered). Shaking does nothing. It replaced held shaking (2026-11-07; backup `versions/pre-tipping` has the short-lived anticlockwise dial, `versions/pre-twist` the shake).
- **Button:** GPIO 0 (`button.h`): the board's BOOT button for now, or pin 12 of the 12-pin header to ground. Holding it is the hold; the drain only rises while it's tipped. Three presses, each within 0.5 s of the last, reset the drain to baseline. Ten minutes without button activity sleeps (never while it's held; movement and touch don't count): the screen goes dark and the blobs stop, while the drain keeps seeping back. One press wakes it and does nothing else. This is not the chip's deep sleep, so it saves the backlight rather than most of the battery. Holding BOOT during power-on or reset enters download mode.
- **Link to the Pi:** `relay_link.h` joins the network in `relay_secrets.h` and connects to the relay at `RELAY_HOST` as population 2 (the blue). Nothing is sent while the button is held: on release the drain level goes out as one quick hold, intensity and release, so the ambient only reacts once the visitor lets go, and the radio is off while asleep. The network, password, relay address and room go in `relay_secrets.h`, which is not in git: copy `relay_secrets.example.h`. Link events print on the serial port as `relay: ...`.
- **Build:** ESP32 board package 3.x, FQBN `esp32:esp32:esp32s3:FlashSize=16M,PSRAM=opi,PartitionScheme=app3M_fat9M_16MB,CDCOnBoot=cdc`. The serial port prints fps and physics load every 2 s; sending `t` tips the plate by 0.5 g to time a pile, `z` sleeps at once, and `b` turns the low-battery ring on or off to test it.

## Checks

`npm test` checks:

- the article's rules: straight gliding with nothing acting, rotation-invariant shape matching and recovery from a squash, damping toward rigid motion, deepest-collision choice, and the exact bounce and friction formulas, with momentum kept;
- device gravity: level and still means settled perfect circles that stay put (also inside the dead zone); a tilt moves every blob; a steady tilt piles them up at rest; level again, they turn round;
- accelerometer-to-screen mapping (upright, sideways, landscape, a push), the press gravity (away from the press, strength, rim cap, easing, Level), and the smoothing that prefers a fresh sensor;
- the press push (stronger closer, none beyond the reach, away from the press) and drag stirring;
- drag and top speed; resting contact; no blob folded inside out under a 1 g pile;
- elasticity at body level, and that inelastic contacts never glue;
- no attraction or repulsion; up to 100 blobs shrinking to fit; a calm 50-blob pile with lone drops still bouncing;
- the shake drain;
- stability and full shape recovery after hard tilting at slider extremes;
- frame-rate consistency, sliders and paired delivery.

Physics takes about 0.8 ms per frame for 50 blobs on a desktop while tilting, 1.9 ms for 100 and 6 ms for 200 (a bounding-circle check skips most point-in-outline tests). The ESP32-S3 will need fewer blobs or a cheaper collision pass. The phone's accelerometer sign convention (W3C) should be checked on an iPhone and an Android phone.
